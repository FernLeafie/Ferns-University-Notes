in the unit circle
$$
\begin{align}
\dfrac{x}{1}=x=\cos v \\
\dfrac{y}{1}=y=\sin v
\end{align}
$$

cos and sin are periodic
$$
\begin{align}
\left\{ \begin{array}{lll}
\cos v=\cos (v+n*2\pi) \\
\sin v=\sin(v+n*2\pi)
\end{array} \right. ,n \in \mathbb{Z} 
\end{align}
$$
the results they give are identical if offset $90\degree$ or $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$
$$
\begin{align}
\left\{ \begin{array}{lll}
\cos\left( \dfrac{\pi}{2}-v \right)=\sin v \\
\sin\left( \dfrac{\pi}{2}-v \right)=\cos v
\end{array} \right. 
\end{align}
$$
this is also clearly visible in triangles
$$
\begin{align}
\cos(\alpha)=\dfrac{b}{c}=\sin\left( \dfrac{\pi}{2}-\alpha \right)=\sin(\beta)
\end{align}
$$


if offset half a turn, the length of the sides are inverted
$$
\begin{align}
\left\{ \begin{array}{lll}
a=\cos v \\
b=\sin v
\end{array} \right. \text{ and }\left\{ \begin{array}{lll}
-a=\cos (v+\pi) \\
-b=\sin (v+\pi)
\end{array} \right. \\
\left\{ \begin{array}{lll}
\cos(v+\pi)=-\cos v \\
\sin(v+\pi)=-\sin v
\end{array} \right.  
\end{align}
$$

trigonometric correlations
pythagorean identity (trigonometric one), oooh this comes from the pythagorean theorem ($a^{2}+b^{2}=c^{2}$) but in the unit circle, so ($a=\cos v,b=\sin v,c=1$)
$$
\begin{align}
\cos^{2}v+\sin ^{2}v=1
\end{align}
$$
addition formulae
$$
\begin{align}
\cos(u-v)=\cos u\cos v+\sin u\sin v \\
\cos(u+v)=\cos u\cos v-\sin u\sin v \\
\sin(u-v)=\sin u\cos v-\cos u\sin v \\
\sin(u+v)=\sin u\cos v+\cos u\sin v
\end{align}
$$
double angle formulae
$$
\begin{align}
\cos2v=\cos ^{2}v-\sin ^{2}v \\
\sin 2v=2\cos v\sin v
\end{align}
$$

since any line not on the exact extremety crosses the circle twice, there's usually two answers when looking for a specific value of sin and cos
cos mirroring along the x axis, and sin mirroring along the y axis
$$
\begin{align}
\cos x=\cos v\iff \left\{ \begin{array}{lll}
x=v&+n*2\pi \\
x=-v&+n*2\pi
\end{array} \right. , n \in \mathbb{Z}
\end{align}
$$

$$
\begin{align}
\sin x=\sin v\iff \left\{ \begin{array}{lll}
x=v&+n*2\pi \\
x=\pi-v&+n*2\pi
\end{array} \right., n \in \mathbb{Z} 
\end{align}
$$

for curves
$$
\begin{align}
y=c\sin(ax+b)+d
\end{align}
$$
$a$ - adjusts the period, higher values mean shorter periods, as the value increases faster, it turns around faster
$b$ - offsets the curve to the left
$c$ - adjusts the amplitude
$d$ - adjusts the vertical offset

tan and cot
$$
\begin{align}
\left\{ \begin{array}{lll}
\tan x=\dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x},x\neq \dfrac{\pi}{2}+n*\pi \\
\cot x=\dfrac{\cos x}{\sin x},x\neq n*\pi
\end{array} \right. , n \in \mathbb{Z}
\end{align}
$$
as opposed to having a period of $2\pi$, tan and cot have a period of $\pi$
$$
\begin{align}
\tan(x+\pi)=\dfrac{\sin(x+\pi)}{\cos(x+\pi)}=\dfrac{-\sin x}{-\cos x}=\dfrac{\sin x}{\cos x}=\tan x
\end{align}
$$

$$
\begin{align}
\left\{ \begin{array}{lll}
\tan(x+n\pi)=\tan x \\
\cot(x+n\pi)=\cot x
\end{array} \right. ,n\in \mathbb{Z}
\end{align}
$$
